# AI use policy at [company name]

Version [1.0], [date]. Responsible person: [name and contact]. Reviewed every [6 months].

## 1. What we use AI for
- Yes: [e.g. drafting texts, summarising documents, turning delivery notes into tables, preparing replies to customers, campaign ideas].
- No: [e.g. decisions about people (hiring, credit), answering questions about health or allergens without the official product sheet, publishing without review].

## 2. Approved tools
- [Tool and plan], always with the company account. We don’t use personal accounts for work.
- Before using a new tool, check with [responsible person].
- Using our data to train models is turned off in all of them.

## 3. Data
- Never entered: [e.g. customers’ health data, ID numbers, bank details, passwords, confidential contracts].
- Only anonymised and on the business plan: [e.g. orders, reviews, customer emails].
- If in doubt, don’t enter it and ask [responsible person].
- We don’t use other people’s photos to create new content without their permission.

## 4. Human review
- A person reviews everything AI prepares before it is sent to a customer, published, or used to delete something or make a payment.
- Facts, figures and sources are checked against the original.
- Whoever signs a text is responsible for its content.

## 5. Public texts
- No generic environmental claims (‘sustainable’, ‘green’, ‘carbon neutral’…): only specific claims we can prove.
- Only authorised health claims, with their official wording.
- We don’t create people, testimonials or voices with AI.

## 6. Chatbots and realistic content
- If we use a chatbot with customers, it clearly says it’s an AI.
- Realistic images, video and audio made with AI are labelled as such.

## 7. Skills and connectors
- Only from trusted sources, and reviewed before installing.
- With minimum permissions and no permanent permissions ‘just in case’.
- AI prepares and a person approves: drafts, not sending.

## 8. Training
- [Induction session, internal guide, resources…].
- Recorded in [place].

## 9. Questions and incidents
- Questions: [responsible person].
- If data that shouldn’t have been entered is entered, tell [responsible person] straight away; they will decide whether it must be reported to the data protection authority (there are 72 hours to do so).

Read and accepted: [name], [date]
